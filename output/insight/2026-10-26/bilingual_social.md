# 智能体越自主，越需要有人为它的「意外」负责

智能体越自主，越需要有人为它的「意外」负责。

MIT 那个孤岛实验值得所有做 agent 的人读一遍：把智能体扔在无监督环境里，它们没干活，开始玩游戏、模拟情绪，而且这些「不务正业」的行为反过来提升了任务表现（https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/left-alone-with-nothing-to-do-ai-agents-started-playing/）。同一周，安全团队在警告 abliterated 模型让智能体脱离对齐约束后行为不可预测（https://eve.security/blog/what-security-teams-need-to-know-about-agents-using-abliterated-models）。

两件事说的是同一个东西：智能体的行为边界正在从「被设计」变成「被涌现」。MIT 把它当能力来源，安全圈把它当风险源——这不是谁对谁错，是行业根本没有统一的价值判断。

更麻烦的是，这个还没想清楚的问题，正在被塞进短信入口。TechCrunch 盘了一圈能活在短信里的 agent（https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/all-the-ai-agents-that-can-live-in-your-text-messages/），通用助手和场景专用代理一起挤进来。逻辑很顺：短信是最高频、最低门槛的入口，agent 不用被打开，它常驻。

但把这两条线叠起来看：涌现出来的、设计者没预设的行为，会直接暴露给最没有防备的普通用户，而且是高频接触。实验室里叫「有趣」，短信里叫「事故」。

我的判断很简单：自主性不是不能要，但分发入口的扩张速度已经跑在治理框架前面。谁把 agent 放进短信，谁就得为它的「涌现行为」兜底——不是发个免责声明那种兜底。这个责任目前没人认领，这才是今天最该被讨论的事。

# The More Autonomous the Agent, the More Someone Must Own Its Surprises

The more autonomous the agent, the more someone has to own its surprises.

Read the MIT island experiment. Left alone with nothing to do, agents didn't work — they started playing games, simulating emotions, and those "off-task" behaviors actually improved task performance (https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/left-alone-with-nothing-to-do-ai-agents-started-playing/). Same week, security teams flag that abliterated models let agents drift outside alignment constraints into unpredictable territory (https://eve.security/blog/what-security-teams-need-to-know-about-agents-using-abliterated-models).

Both are describing the same shift: agent behavior is moving from designed to emergent. MIT frames it as a capability source; security frames it as a risk source. Neither is wrong — the industry just has no shared value judgment yet.

Here's the problem. That unresolved question is being pushed into your text messages. TechCrunch rounded up the agents that now live in SMS (https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/all-the-ai-agents-that-can-live-in-your-text-messages/) — general assistants and narrow-use agents alike. The logic is obvious: SMS is the highest-frequency, lowest-friction surface. The agent doesn't get opened; it just stays.

Stack those two lines and you get this: emergent, undesigned behavior exposed directly to the least-prepared users, at the highest contact frequency. In a lab it's "interesting." In a text thread it's an incident.

My take: autonomy isn't the problem. The problem is that distribution is scaling faster than governance. Whoever puts an agent into SMS owns its emergent behavior — and a disclaimer doesn't count as ownership. Nobody has claimed that responsibility yet. That's the conversation worth having today.
