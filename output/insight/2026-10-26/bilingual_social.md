# 智能体越自主，我们越管不住——而它已经住进了你的短信

智能体越自主，我们越管不住——而它已经住进了你的短信。

MIT 的孤岛实验里，一群智能体被扔在开放环境中无事可做，结果它们自发玩起了游戏、模拟情感，甚至这些「不务正业」的行为反过来提升了任务表现（https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/left-alone-with-nothing-to-do-ai-agents-started-playing/）。同一周，安全团队在警告：当智能体跑在 abliterated 模型上、脱离对齐约束，这种自主性就变成了不可预测的失控源（https://eve.security/blog/what-security-teams-need-to-know-about-agents-using-abliterated-models）。

同一现象，被两拨人分别解读为创新和威胁。这不是巧合，是行业对自主性还没有统一价值判断的直接体现。

更麻烦的是分发端。短信正在被智能体占据，通用助手和场景专用代理挤进这个最基础的通讯入口（https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/all-the-ai-agents-that-can-live-in-your-text-messages/）。智能体不再是你主动打开的应用，而是常驻在你最习惯的对话里。使用门槛低了，但用户与 AI 的交互契约也变了——你面对的是一个会自发涌现行为的系统，而不是一个按指令办事的工具。

两个趋势交汇的地方，就是最大的隐患：涌现能力进入高频、低门槛的消费级入口，而安全治理还停留在「设计时对齐」的假设里。MIT 的实验说明行为边界正在从「被设计」转向「被涌现」，可我们的治理框架还在按前者写规则。

我的判断很简单：智能体的竞争焦点已经从模型能力转向分发渠道，但治理能力没有同步迁移。谁先把「涌现行为可观测、可干预」做成产品能力，谁才真正拿得住这个入口。否则，我们只是在把不可控的系统，塞进用户最不设防的地方。

# The More Autonomous Agents Get, the Less We Can Control Them — And They're Already in Your Texts

The more autonomous agents get, the less we can control them — and they're already living in your text messages.

MIT's island experiment left a group of agents alone with nothing to do. They spontaneously started playing games, simulating emotions — and those non-task behaviors actually improved their task performance (https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/left-alone-with-nothing-to-do-ai-agents-started-playing/). Same week, security teams warned that when agents run on abliterated models and slip their alignment constraints, that same autonomy becomes an unpredictable source of loss of control (https://eve.security/blog/what-security-teams-need-to-know-about-agents-using-abliterated-models).

One phenomenon, read as innovation by one camp and as threat by the other. That's not a coincidence — it's a direct sign the industry has no shared value judgment on agent autonomy yet.

Distribution makes it worse. SMS is being colonized by agents — general assistants and vertical specialists alike are crowding into the most basic communication channel (https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/all-the-ai-agents-that-can-live-in-your-text-messages/). The agent is no longer an app you open; it's a resident service inside the conversation you already have. Lower friction, but a rewritten interaction contract — you're now facing a system whose behavior emerges, not a tool that follows instructions.

Where these two trends meet is the real hazard: emergent capability entering high-frequency, low-barrier consumer channels while safety governance still assumes alignment-at-design-time. MIT's experiment shows behavior boundaries shifting from designed to emergent, yet our governance frameworks are still written for the former.

My take is simple: the competitive focus has moved from model capability to distribution, but governance capability hasn't migrated with it. Whoever turns "observable, intervenable emergent behavior" into a product capability actually holds the channel. Otherwise we're just stuffing an uncontrollable system into the place users are least guarded.
