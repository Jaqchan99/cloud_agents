# The Agent Race Has Moved from Models to Harnesses. Still Counting Parameters?

Stop obsessing over model leaderboards. Today, Nvidia dropped research that says it plainly: the harness—the engineering layer that connects models to the real world—is the real hero. The same day, Autolith launched a programming agent with a live runtime, Slack unveiled collaborative vibe-coding channels, and Munder Difflin built an 'office of your clones' for multi-agent collaboration. The signal is unmistakable: the agent race has shifted from 'who has the smarter model' to 'who has the sturdier engineering.'

Think about it. When model capabilities converge, what actually decides the winner? It's whether an agent can reliably execute multi-step tasks, stay controllable in messy real-world environments, and collaborate seamlessly with other agents. Nvidia's research proves it empirically: the same model, wrapped in a well-designed harness, can perform dramatically better. That's not theory—that's today's headline.

Meanwhile, Slack and Munder Difflin are showing the next layer: agents are no longer personal tools; they're becoming team infrastructure. Slack turns vibe-coding into shared channels; Munder Difflin lets you and your clones hold meetings, write code, and make decisions together. This isn't 'AI helps me work' anymore—it's 'AI works with me,' or even 'AI works by itself.'

Of course, the route forks here: Inherent's DeepMind alumni are betting on specialized agents as 'teammates' outperforming generalists, while Munder Difflin goes for a universal multi-agent framework. Specialized vs. general—that's the debate to watch in the coming year. But either way, the bottleneck is the same: the harness and collaboration layer determine the ceiling.

If you're still comparing parameter counts, you're already behind. The real race is happening outside the model.
