# Agent越强，人类越瞎？

今天AI圈最值得注意的不是Qwen又登顶了，也不是Meta又发了个编程Agent——而是这三件事放在一起看，暴露了一个危险的失衡。

一边，Agent能力在狂飙：Qwen3.8 Max在Agentic Index上登顶（https://artificialanalysis.ai/?intelligence=agentic-index），Meta推出Muse Code专攻大型代码库（https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/05/meta-launches-muse-code-an-ai-agent-for-large-code-bases/），Prime Agent甚至开始用强化学习自我改进（https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/prime-agent）。模型厂商们把Agent能力当作军备竞赛的核心指标，一天一个突破。

另一边，安全监督还停留在石器时代。ScaleX的研究显示，人类在4万次游戏运行中审批Agent指令时，漏掉了三分之一的威胁（https://scalex.dev/blog/ai-agent-permissions-stats/）。三分之一啊。这意味着你部署的Agent每执行三次高危操作，就有一次是在人类眼皮底下溜过去的。

更魔幻的是，Google Maps已经开始把点餐、订酒店这些真实世界任务交给Agent直接执行（https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/05/google-maps-adds-agentic-features-including-food-ordering-and-hotel-bookings/）。普通用户根本不会像技术人员那样警惕，他们只会觉得“这玩意儿真方便”。当Agent能替你做决定、花你的钱、操作你的账号时，那三分之一的人类监督漏洞就不再是研究数据，而是真实事故。

我的观点很明确：现在Agent能力竞赛的方向错了。与其比谁的模型能更快完成复杂任务，不如先解决“人类如何可靠地监督Agent”这个问题。否则，我们就是在给一辆没有刹车的车装更大马力的引擎。等到消费级Agent真正铺开的那天，今天的三分之一漏洞就是明天的头条新闻。

# The Stronger the Agent, the Blinder the Human?

The most telling news today isn't Qwen topping another benchmark or Meta shipping another coding agent. It's what happens when you put three stories side by side: a dangerous imbalance.

On one side, agent capabilities are exploding. Qwen3.8 Max just topped the Agentic Index (https://artificialanalysis.ai/?intelligence=agentic-index), Meta launched Muse Code for large codebases (https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/05/meta-launches-muse-code-an-ai-agent-for-large-code-bases/), and Prime Agent is even exploring self-improvement via reinforcement learning (https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/prime-agent). Every vendor is treating agentic ability as the new battleground metric, and they're shipping breakthroughs daily.

On the other side, safety oversight is stuck in the Stone Age. ScaleX's research found that humans reviewing agent commands across 40,000 game runs missed one in three threats (https://scalex.dev/blog/ai-agent-permissions-stats/). One in three. That means for every three high-risk actions your agent executes, one slips past human review unnoticed.

And then there's Google Maps quietly rolling out agentic features that let AI order food and book hotels directly (https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/05/google-maps-adds-agentic-features-including-food-ordering-and-hotel-bookings/). Regular users won't be as vigilant as engineers. They'll just think 'this is convenient.' When agents can make decisions, spend money, and act on your behalf in the real world, that one-in-three oversight gap stops being a research finding and becomes a headline.

My take is simple: the current direction of the agent capability race is wrong. Instead of competing over who can complete more complex tasks faster, we should first solve the problem of how humans can reliably supervise agents. Otherwise, we're bolting a bigger engine onto a car with no brakes. By the time consumer agents are everywhere, today's one-in-three gap will be tomorrow's front-page story.
