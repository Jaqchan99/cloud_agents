# Agent 的军备竞赛已转移：模型不再是主角，工程化才是

别再看模型参数了。今天 Nvidia 的研究把话挑明了：Agent 的真正瓶颈不在模型，而在 harness——那个外部框架。模型是引擎，但框架是方向盘和刹车。没有好的 harness，再强的模型也只是一匹脱缰的野马。

这不是纸上谈兵。开源社区已经在行动：有人搞出了近乎完全自托管的 Agentic 软件工厂，Proliferate 直接开源了一个“任何编程 Agent 都能用的 Codex”。这些项目在证明一件事：谁掌握框架和工具链，谁就掌握 Agent 的生产力。

而另一边，商业化的步子迈得更大。币安直接让 AI Agent 进场交易，但把风控责任甩给用户——这就像把火箭交给实习生，然后说“你自己看着办”。Slack 倒是聪明，把 vibe-coding 做成协作频道，让团队监督 Agent 的行为。两种路径，两种对失控的态度：一个推给个人，一个靠组织兜底。

但问题在于：框架的成熟和商业化的速度根本不在一个量级。我们还没搞清怎么可靠地隔离、调度 Agent，就已经让它们碰真金白银了。这不是“先跑起来再修”的问题，这是把安全气囊拆了上赛道。

框架是地基，商业化是高楼。地基还没打牢，楼已经盖到十层了。行业该停下来想想：我们是不是跑得太快了？

# The Agent Arms Race Has Shifted: Models Are No Longer the Hero, Engineering Is

Stop obsessing over model parameters. Nvidia's latest research makes it clear: the real bottleneck for AI agents isn't the model—it's the harness. The model is the engine, but the harness is the steering wheel and the brakes. Without a solid harness, even the most powerful model is just a runaway horse.

This isn't theoretical. The open-source community is already acting on it: someone built a nearly fully self-hosted agentic software factory, and Proliferate open-sourced a "Codex for any coding agent." These projects prove one thing: whoever controls the framework and toolchain controls agent productivity.

Meanwhile, commercialization is sprinting ahead. Binance is letting AI agents trade directly, but shifting risk responsibility to users—like handing a rocket to an intern and saying "figure it out." Slack is smarter: they turned vibe-coding into collaborative channels, letting teams supervise agent behavior. Two approaches, two attitudes toward loss-of-control: one pushes it to individuals, the other relies on organizational oversight.

But here's the disconnect: framework maturity and commercialization speed are on completely different timelines. We haven't figured out how to reliably isolate and orchestrate agents, yet we're already letting them touch real money. This isn't "move fast and fix later"—this is hitting the track without airbags.

Frameworks are the foundation; commercialization is the skyscraper. The foundation isn't set, but we're already ten floors up. The industry needs to ask itself: are we moving too fast?
