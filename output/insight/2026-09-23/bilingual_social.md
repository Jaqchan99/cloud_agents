# Muse 跑得越快，越像一颗定时炸弹

Muse 跑得越快，越像一颗定时炸弹。

今天三条新闻放在一起看，Meta 的处境其实很尴尬：Muse 的移动端下载量已经超过 ChatGPT 同期（TechCrunch），但同一天，安全研究员扒出了 Muse 的严重 0-day，攻击者可以借此直接控制这个「权限高得离谱」的 AI 助手（Ars Technica），Meta 只能被动打补丁（The Verge）。更早一点，亚马逊直接封了 Muse 的代购行为（The Verge）。

这三件事不是三个独立问题。Muse 之所以起量快，是因为 Meta 给了它极高的系统权限和社交生态入口；而正是这个「高权限 + 快速铺开」的组合，同时制造了安全漏洞和平台反弹。换句话说，Muse 的增长引擎和它的风险源是同一个东西。

联合国今天发报告说 AI 保障不能等确定性（The Verge），呼吁各国政府按预防原则尽早介入。但 Meta 的漏洞是被研究员发现后才补的——这就是监管层说的「预防」和产业界实际做的「事后补课」之间的真实落差。

真正值得警惕的不是某个漏洞本身，而是：当 Agent 开始跨越平台边界执行任务，平台方（亚马逊）、监管方（联合国）、安全方（研究员）会从三个方向同时收紧。Muse 的 C 端成功，不等于它拿到了生态通行证。

Agent 赛道的下一个分水岭，不是模型能力，而是谁先建立起「权限可控、行为可审计、跨平台可协商」的治理层。谁先解决这个，谁才真正跑得远。

# Muse Is Winning the Race It Can't Afford to Win

Muse is winning a race it can't afford to win.

Three stories today, one uncomfortable picture for Meta. Muse is outpacing ChatGPT's early mobile launch (TechCrunch). Same day, researchers disclose a serious 0-day in Muse — an AI assistant with "extraordinarily privileged" system access — letting attackers take control of the agent (Ars Technica). Meta patches it after the fact (The Verge). And Amazon has already blocked Muse from acting as a shopping agent on its platform (The Verge).

These aren't three separate problems. Muse grows fast because Meta gave it deep system permissions and a social-graph on-ramp. That same combination — high privilege plus rapid rollout — is what produced both the vulnerability and the platform backlash. The growth engine and the risk surface are the same object.

The UN published a report today arguing AI safeguards can't wait for certainty, urging governments to act on precautionary principle (The Verge). Meanwhile Meta's fix came only after a researcher found the hole. That gap — between the regulator's "act early" and the industry's "patch later" — is the real story.

The thing to watch isn't any single CVE. It's that once agents start crossing platform boundaries to execute tasks, platforms (Amazon), regulators (UN), and security researchers will all tighten simultaneously. Consumer traction is not ecosystem permission.

The next dividing line in agents isn't model capability. It's who builds the governance layer first — controllable permissions, auditable behavior, negotiable cross-platform access. Whoever solves that is the one who actually gets to scale.
