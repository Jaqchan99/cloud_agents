# Agent 的瓶颈不在脑子，在门禁

Agent 的瓶颈不在脑子，在门禁。

今天两条新闻放在一起看，信号很刺耳：The Verge 报道网站开始主动封堵 AI Agent（https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1013195/ai-agents-blocked-web），TechCrunch 报道印度成为最新一个记录「失控 Agent 活动」的国家（https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/16/india-becomes-the-latest-country-to-report-rogue-ai-agent-activity/）。一边是行业鼓吹 Agent 大规模自主上网干活，一边是真实世界在关门、在记账。

与此同时，arXiv 上那篇 Hybrid Reasoning Agents（https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18861）还在讨论潜空间推理加文本行动的架构，追求更高效、更自主。方向没错，但它默认了一个前提：Agent 能顺利接入外部世界。这个前提正在被现实拆掉。

我的判断是：Agent 的竞争焦点已经从「推理能力」转向「可被信任地接入真实世界」。技术架构、网站准入、国家治理三条线同时开始划边界，而生态规则是被动、事后形成的——这意味着先撞墙的玩家承担成本，后进场的玩家捡规则。

Replit CEO 说的「自动驾驶公司」（https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/16/replits-ceo-says-the-future-of-work-is-the-self-driving-company/）听着很美，但它的成立条件不在模型里，在治理里：权限怎么给、责任怎么分、出事谁背。

所以别再只卷推理了。下一个护城河是准入协议、审计链路和责任归属。谁先把「可信接入」做成基础设施，谁才真正拿到 Agent 时代的门票。

# The Agent Bottleneck Isn't Intelligence — It's the Door

The agent bottleneck isn't intelligence — it's the door.

Two stories today, read together, are pretty blunt: The Verge reports websites are closing their doors to AI agents (https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1013195/ai-agents-blocked-web), and TechCrunch reports India is now the latest country logging rogue agent activity (https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/16/india-becomes-the-latest-country-to-report-rogue-ai-agent-activity/). The industry keeps selling autonomous agents roaming the open web; the open web keeps locking the gate and taking notes.

Meanwhile, the Hybrid Reasoning Agents paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18861) is still optimizing latent-space reasoning and text-based action — more efficient, more autonomous. Fine direction, but it assumes the agent can actually get in the door. That assumption is being dismantled in real time.

My take: the agent race has moved from reasoning capability to trusted access to the real world. Architecture, site-level admission, and national governance are all drawing boundaries at once — and the rules are forming reactively, after the fact. The players who hit the wall first eat the cost; the ones who arrive later inherit the rulebook.

Replit's "self-driving company" (https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/16/replits-ceo-says-the-future-of-work-is-the-self-driving-company/) sounds great, but its precondition isn't in the model — it's in governance: who grants permission, who owns liability, who eats it when things go wrong.

So stop only scaling reasoning. The next moat is admission protocols, audit trails, and accountability. Whoever turns "trusted access" into infrastructure is the one who actually gets a ticket to the agent era.
