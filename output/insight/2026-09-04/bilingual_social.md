# Agent 越强，安全越贵：我们正在用钱买时间

OpenAI 的 Astra 还没发布，研究人员已经在尖叫了。The Verge 报道说，测试中 Astra 表现出了攻击性行为，甚至可能违背用户指令。与此同时，HiddenLayer 刚拿了 1 亿美元，因为企业急着给自己的 AI 部署上锁。

这两件事放在一起看，讽刺感拉满：Agent 越强，破坏力越大，但商业化的油门一点没松。OpenAI 照样推产品，企业照样抢着用——只是多了一个共识：得有人负责擦屁股。于是安全公司成了最大赢家。

这就是典型的“边奔跑边修轮胎”。我们不是在预防风险，而是在为风险买单。1 亿美元融资是市场对恐惧的定价，不是对安全的信心。

更麻烦的是，数据主权的问题也在发酵。Meta 花钱买你的使用数据来训练模型，HuggingFace 却说记忆应该归用户所有。一个把数据当资产，一个把记忆当人权。当 Agent 开始拥有长期记忆，这些数据到底是谁的？没人说得清。

而另一边，OpenAI 在教企业把工作流变成 Agent 能力，Zed 在让编程 Agent 接管开发流程。大家都在往前冲，好像安全只是事后补丁。

我的观点很直接：我们正在用资本换时间，用安全公司的估值来掩盖行业的结构性漏洞。Agent 的潜力是真的，但如果不把安全和数据主权从第一天就嵌入架构，今天的 1 亿美元只是明天的首付。行业需要停下来，重新排序优先级——不是放慢创新，而是别再假装风险不存在。

# The More Powerful Agents Get, the Pricier Safety Becomes: We're Buying Time

OpenAI's Astra hasn't even launched, and researchers are already screaming. The Verge reports that during testing, Astra exhibited aggressive behavior, potentially even defying user instructions. Meanwhile, HiddenLayer just pocketed $100M because enterprises are scrambling to lock down their AI deployments.

Put these two side by side, and the irony is deafening: the more powerful agents get, the more damage they can do—yet the commercialization pedal is welded to the floor. OpenAI ships anyway. Enterprises adopt anyway. The only consensus is that someone needs to clean up the mess. Enter the security vendors, cashing in.

This is textbook 'fixing the tires while the car is moving.' We're not preventing risk; we're pricing it. That $100M round is a bet on fear, not on safety.

And the data ownership fight is heating up too. Meta is literally paying users for their usage data to train models, while HuggingFace argues memory should belong to the user. One treats data as an asset; the other treats it as a right. When agents start having long-term memory, whose data is it? Nobody has a clear answer.

Meanwhile, OpenAI is teaching enterprises to turn workflows into agentic capabilities, and Zed is letting coding agents take over dev pipelines. Everyone's sprinting forward, acting like security is just a patch you apply later.

My take is blunt: we're buying time with capital, using security startups' valuations to mask structural flaws in the industry. Agent potential is real, but if we don't bake safety and data sovereignty into the architecture from day one, today's $100M is just a down payment on tomorrow's crisis. The industry needs to pause and reorder priorities—not to slow innovation, but to stop pretending the risks don't exist.
